VESTA

Autonomous drone docking platform for coast guard operations

Project Type

Academic / Industry Collaboration
Unibo × Almacube
Bologna / Ferrara

Problem & Target

Sea waves & wind, hazardous landings, 35-min battery limit.
Target: Firefighters, Coast Guard, Navy units.

Objectives

Safe autonomous landing, instant structural locking, infinite mission range via continuous battery-swap cycle.

Role

Lead Product Designer
CAD Modeling & Visual Communication
Team Intermediary

Supervisors

Matteo Vignoli
Giuseppe Mincolelli
Bernardo Balboni

A self-leveling platform engineered for autonomous drone operations at sea.

VESTA was born from a real operational challenge faced by the Italian Coast Guard: DJI drones used in rescue operations face a hard 35-minute flight limit due to battery constraints. We designed a platform that solves this with a continuous battery-swap cycle — while the drone is operational, a secondary battery recharges on the vessel, enabling an uninterrupted, theoretically infinite mission cycle.

The platform uses switchable permanent magnets triggered by a servomotor via Arduino and Wi-Fi. The magnets activate automatically just before touchdown, instantly locking the drone's landing gear. An Aruco marker tracking system guides autonomous docking, with physical tests confirming the UAV successfully tracks and follows the moving marker, ensuring precise touchdown even on moving vessels.

From Concept to Prototype: Our first concept used three linear pistons driven by threaded shafts to level the platform — testing revealed it was too slow for wave frequency. The final solution implements a spherical mechanism with 3D-printed curved arms and three Arduino-controlled servomotors, achieving the fast and reliable response needed in real sea conditions.

Key Technologies

  • Aruco Marker Tracking: Autonomous drone guidance
  • Switchable Magnets: Servomotor-triggered via Arduino & Wi-Fi
  • Spherical Mechanism: 3-servo adaptive platform leveling
  • Battery Swap Cycle: Continuous, infinite mission capability

Prototyping

The team 3D-printed curved arms connected to three Arduino-controlled servomotors. Physical sea-condition simulations confirmed stable performance and reliable autonomous drone docking under wave motion.
