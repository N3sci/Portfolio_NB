Connecting generations through creativity, play, and shared doing.

A Mano A Mano is an interior design project developed as a thesis in the final design studio for Product and Interior Design. The project responds to a pressing social issue: in Italy, 70% of elderly people do not participate in group activities, and 2 out of 10 have no contact with other people during a typical week. By 2050, over 34.5% of the Italian population will be over 65.

The goal is to create interior design solutions and organize services that involve and connect different users — primarily the elderly (Over 65) and children — through the central theme of "doing": creativity, play, storytelling, and discovery, activities that are natural bridges between generations.

Research phase: The project was grounded in extensive field research at leading intergenerational centers: Reggio Children (Malaguzzi Center), ArcaLab in Florence, Hotel Adda in Paderno d'Adda, and ABI Piacenza. This direct contact shaped every design decision.

The Three Ateliers

Wind Atelier

Inspired by the Adriatic winds of Romagna. A space for kite-making, textile work, and light-material crafting — activities that connect the feeling of outdoor freedom with indoor creativity.

Adriatic Observatory

Reflecting Rimini's relationship with the sea. A contemplative space for storytelling, map exploration, and observational activities, designed to stimulate memory and imagination across generations.

Creative Lab

A hands-on workshop for material exploration, painting, and sculpture. Shared tables and dedicated spaces encourage parallel yet connected activity between elderly participants and children.

Design Principles

  • Shared common tables & dedicated personal spaces
  • Leftover materials collection & reuse
  • Ergonomic study of seating for elderly & children
  • Lightweight tables, chairs, and modular shelves
  • Designed for proximity & shared activity

Field Research

Research was conducted at four leading centers: Reggio Children (Malaguzzi), ArcaLab Florence, Hotel Adda Paderno d'Adda, and ABI Piacenza. Direct observation and interviews informed every design choice from spatial layout to furniture ergonomics.
